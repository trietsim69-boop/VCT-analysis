# Budget Cost Research: VCT Duelist Signing for Sentinels (2027)

**Date compiled:** 2026-09-29 (research agent). Spot-checked after review: the THESPIKE $600K–$5M range and its make-up, Riot declining to state a 2027 minimum, and the Americas Stage 2 2026 prize table all match their sources.
**Shortlist (T7 fit score, "Top 10 of 84" band):** Meiy, swagzor, Derke, ZmjjKK, primmie, BuZz, dgzin, OXY, Wo0t, Timotino. aspas, at 11th, is included in §7 for reference.
**Purpose:** Public anchors for the labelled assumptions in the "Which duelist should Sentinels sign for 2027, and what is it worth paying?" budget model. This is an unofficial portfolio analysis. Every figure below is an anchor for an assumption. None of them is a known SEN contract term.

**FX used (ECB reference rates, 29 Sep 2026) [36]:** EUR/USD 1.1355; EUR/KRW 1536.74, so USD/KRW ≈ 1,353.4; EUR/CAD 1.6101, so USD/CAD ≈ 1.418.

## Summary: what can and cannot be known

Riot's own pages cover the **structure** of team revenue well: the 2027 partner benefits (base payment + performance bonus + team capsules), the non-partner qualification payouts, the capsule revenue share, and the 2025 totals ($105.2M shared, $86M from digital goods, stated in Leo Faria's letter). Esports Earnings and VLR give reliable **prize pools**. Sentinels' **SEC Form C-AR** is a real primary source for org-level revenue and losses. **Player salaries, buyouts and agent fees are not disclosed** in VALORANT. The only numbers are the Riot-mandated 2023 minimums (primary document reachable only through Liquipedia, which was rate-limited during this session), a small number of named-person claims (coaches, CEOs, streamers), and journalist reports that rely on anonymous sources. The $600K–$5M/yr range for 2027 partners was reported by THESPIKE, citing Riot's meetings with teams. I could not find it on a Riot page, because Riot's own 2027 announcement article sits on X (robots-blocked) and the valorantesports.com version rendered empty. Salary, buyout, agent-fee, slot-freeing and sponsorship-uplift anchors should therefore be treated as **illustrative ranges bracketed by the sparse reported cases**, and the partnership and prize anchors as **well-grounded**.

## 1. Minimum salaries

- **VCT 2023 partnered leagues:** Americas **USD 50,000/yr**, EMEA **EUR 50,000/yr** (≈ USD 56,775), Pacific **KRW 67,000,000/yr** (≈ USD 49,506). The minimum is base salary only. Streaming money, prize money and performance bonuses do not count toward it. Source: Riot's *VCT23 Roster Construction Rules*, as summarised by Dexerto on 28 Sep 2022 (updated 31 Oct 2022) [1]. The Riot PDF is hosted on Liquipedia [2], which returned HTTP 429 every time, so I could not read it directly. **Pre-2024: possibly outdated.**
- Named-person corroboration: the Global Esports CEO (Rushindra Sinha) publicly stated a **USD 50,000 minimum per player** for VCT Pacific 2023 (~$350k for a 7-player roster) and ~$2M first-year total expense [3].
- **2024 / 2025 changes:** Riot's VCT24 and VCT25 Roster Construction Rules exist as PDFs on Liquipedia [4][5] but could not be fetched (429). Dexerto's summary of the 2024 rule changes (6 Sep 2023) lists transfer-window, roster-size, two-way and safe-harbor changes and **no change to minimum salary** [6]. A 2023 esports.net piece says "$50,000 per year for American players" still applied for 2024+ [7].
- **2026:** Riot's 2026 VCT Official Competition Ruleset is listed in the Riot Competitive Ops Library (last updated 18 Dec 2025) [8], but the Sanity CDN PDF is robots-blocked. I found no evidence that the figure changed.
- **2027:** THESPIKE (17 Apr 2026) reports that Riot **declined to say whether minimum player salaries will be mandated** under the 2027 partnership [9]. Nothing on Riot's 2027 pages I read [10] mentions a salary floor.
- **Challengers / Ascension minimums:** I found no public Riot figure. Forum claims exist (e.g. Korea VCL ≈ KRW 20M/yr), but they are unverified **rumour** [11].
- Cross-reference: **LEC minimum EUR 60,000/yr** (≈ USD 68,130), per Riot's LEC rulebook as reported by Sheep Esports (17 Jan 2025) [12].

**Confidence:** Primary rule (2023) via secondary summary plus a named CEO confirmation. 2024–2026 continuation: secondary / inferred. 2027: not found (Riot declined).

## 2. Typical VCT player salaries

- **2021–22 NA:** "the average Valorant pro salary is between US$5,000 and US$20,000 a month" (≈ **$60k–$240k/yr**), said on stream by ShahZaM (then SEN IGL, Feb 2022) and by Shroud (Aug 2021) [13][14]. Kyedae said on stream that nitr0 and sinatraa earned **$25,000/month** (100T/SEN, Apr 2021) [13]. **Pre-2024: outdated, pre-franchising peak.**
- **2023 NA:** $20,000–$30,000/month (≈ $240k–$360k/yr) was typical before the 2024 cuts. Cloud9 reportedly paid yay **$60,000/month** (~$720k/yr, an outlier). These claims come from EG coach potter and Giants player rhyme, as reported by esports.net (10 Aug 2023) [7]. The same piece says 100T and C9 planned to cut player pay for 2024, and that NA pay was "five times" EMEA pay. **Secondary-reported, pre-2024.**
- **EG (reigning world champions) 2023:** EG reportedly offered players a **50% pay cut** for 2024. Reported by Richard Lewis via Dexerto (23 Sep 2023) [15].
- **TenZ:** a "seven-figure deal" with Sentinels in June 2021 (multi-year and unspecified; ONE Esports) [13]. No credible annual figure was found.
- **aspas, Derke, Demon1, zekken and the other shortlisted duelists:** **no credibly reported salary figures found.**
- **Direction of travel:** Sentinels' 2025 Form C-AR shows operating expenses down from $11.19M (2024) to $9.82M (2025). CEO Rob Moore says compensation was restructured "for both staff and players" [16][17]. The same filing discloses officer base salaries (CEO $360k, COO $215k, CMO $215k, CRO $200k) [16]. These give a useful sense of scale, since a star player is unlikely to cost many times the CEO's pay.
- **Cross-reference, LoL:** LEC average **EUR 240,000** (≈ USD 272,520) and median **EUR 165,000** (≈ USD 187,358) in Winter 2025, down from EUR 270k in Summer 2024. Rookies average EUR 115k. The average team spends EUR 1.2M on its active roster. Source: Sheep Esports, anonymous sources, claimed ±EUR 20k accuracy [12]. The **LCS** average fell below the LEC average in 2025. Inspired, the top LCS earner, is estimated at **>USD 500,000/yr** in 2026 [18]. **Secondary-reported (anonymous sources).**
- **Cross-reference, CS2:** I found no reliable per-player salary figure (the bo3.gg article did not render).

**Confidence:** Secondary-reported or named-person claims only. No primary salary data exists publicly.

## 3. Buyouts and transfer fees

- **TenZ, Cloud9 → Sentinels (Jun 2021):** **USD 1.25M buyout**, reported by Inven Global. A USD 2M buyout clause had been rumoured [19]. **Pre-2024 and pre-franchising, so it marks the peak of the market.**
- **Ethan and Demon1, EG → NRG (2023–24):** EG reportedly rejected a **USD 100,000** buyout offer (Richard Lewis via Dexerto) [15][20]. Former NRG coach Chet Singh said he personally contributed "low five figures" toward the eventual buyouts (Dexerto, 16 Jul 2024) [20]. The players were reigning world champions with about one contract year left. **Secondary-reported, 2023–24.**
- **aspas, Leviatán → MIBR (Oct 2024), zekken, SEN → MIBR (late 2025), and SEN's buys of johnqt, N4RRATE, Jerrwin and Reduxx:** reported as agreements, but **no fee disclosed** [21][22]. Sheep Esports reported in 2026 that aspas stays with MIBR.
- **CS2 benchmarks:** m0NESY to Falcons **≈ USD 2–2.5M** (claimed by analyst OverDrive and "rumored"). kyousuke, Spirit Academy → Falcons (Jun 2025), **reported ~USD 2M** by OverDrive and disputed by Falcons' chairman [23]. CS2 has an open circuit with much larger third-party prize and sponsor money, so treat these figures as a **ceiling analogy**.
- **Contract length as a driver:** Riot publishes a **Global Contract Database** (contract end dates, residency status and a team contact). It is official, per Sheep Esports (17 Jul 2024) [24], but it is a Google Sheet (docs.google.com is blocked). VLR, citing that database, reports that johnqt, Reduxx, cortezia, JonahP and Jerrwin all hold SEN contracts **through 2028**, and that SEN let Jerrwin explore options on 15 Sep 2026 [25]. Buyout clauses are typically "a set fee or amount" written into the contract (Esports Legal News, 2024) [26]. No public formula links fee to years remaining. The EG case shows that a team with leverage can refuse even a reasonable offer ("contract jail").

**Confidence:** Secondary-reported (single cases). The general rule linking remaining contract to fee is not found in any source and remains an illustrative assumption.

## 4. Agent fees

- British Esports (UK governing body) careers page, updated Jun 2025: "Agents in Europe can typically take **10–15% commission** per sale" [27].
- Esportsadvokaten (Danish esports law firm) warns about predatory agent contracts that take **5–50% of monthly income** for 2–4-year non-cancellable terms. Commission is paid by the player from salary [28].
- Named VALORANT agencies (Prodigy Agency representing TenZ and ScreaM; agent Jérôme Coupez) appear in a Nerd Street piece (Aug 2022), which gives no percentages [29].
- **FIFA analogy (labelled analogy only):** FIFA Football Agent Regulations (effective 1 Oct 2023) cap fees at **5% of player remuneration ≤ $200k/yr, and 3% above that**. The same caps apply to an agent acting for the hiring club (10%/6% with dual representation), and **10% of the transfer fee** applies for the selling club [30]. Parts of the cap have since been challenged in European courts [30].

**Confidence:** Secondary / industry body (10–15%). Esports-specific practice varies, and a player-paid commission is not a team cost unless the team grosses it up.

## 5. Import rule and freeing an import slot

- **Riot VCT 2023 rule (partnered leagues):** only **one non-resident (import)** on the starting roster, so 4 of 5 players must be residents. A resident holds citizenship, lawful permanent residency, or refugee/asylum status in the league territory. Game Changers alumni can claim residency, and dual citizens choose one league [1]. The **Americas territory spans NA, Brazil and LATAM**, so for SEN a Brazilian or LATAM duelist is **not** an import.
- **2026:** For Challengers / Path to Champions, Riot's own 2026 post says teams must field "3 players from their region and 2 players globally", with up to two imports allowed in some qualifying contexts [31]. I could not verify from a readable Riot document whether the partnered-league rule for 2026–27 is still one import, because the VCT25 rules PDF (Liquipedia) returned 429 and the 2026 ruleset PDF is robots-blocked. **For 2027 the format moves to open tournaments, and the import rule is not published in anything I could read.**
- **How teams free a slot:** (a) sell or transfer the import to another team, which may *bring in* a fee; (b) loan them out; (c) bench them or place them on reserve while paying salary; (d) mutual release, paying out part or all of the remaining contract. SEN has recently used (c)/(d)-style moves, for example allowing Jerrwin (contract to 2028) to explore free agency [25]. **No public figures exist for payouts on release.** VLR reports johnqt's contract runs to 2028 [25]. I did not verify from a primary source whether johnqt occupies SEN's import slot (the input premise).

- **Project note (added after review):** this repo already confirms the premise. The T2 read of Riot's Global Contract Database (`gcd_2026-09-14.xlsx`, AMERICAS tab) lists johnqt as **Non-Resident, contract to 2028**. That is assumption S-10 in `docs/assumptions_log.md`. Still unverified: whether the one-import limit carries into 2027.

**Confidence:** 2023 rule: primary via secondary summary. 2026–27 partnered rule: not verified. Slot-freeing cost: not found, so illustrative.

## 6. Team revenue from Riot

- **Original partnership stipend (2023 cycle):** **USD 600,000 minimum to USD 1.5M/yr**, varying by region (lower in Asia), performance, league revenue and deliverables. Reported by Dexerto from multiple unnamed sources (11 Aug 2022) [32]. **Pre-2024.**
- **2024 composition (Sheep Esports, 6 Aug 2024, sources plus a Riot spokesperson):** league participation stipend **~USD 300k**, a digital-goods guarantee of **USD 300k** (fixed at **USD 400k** for 2025), and a content-creation incentive of **up to USD 400k/team**, which was cut for 2025. The Champions bundle shares **50% of revenue in 60 parts**: all 44 clubs get 1 part and the 16 Champions qualifiers get 2 [33].
- **Team capsules:** Riot shares **50% of gross sales after hard costs** (production, taxes, platform and transaction fees). Leo Faria told Digiday this on 5 Mar 2024 [34]. The Americas top-selling capsules in 2025 were LEV #1, G2 #2 and **SEN #3**, down from **#1 in 2024** (Sheep, citing Riot) [35].
- **Totals shared with teams:** **2024: USD 78.4M** to 44 teams, of which **USD 44.3M came from digital goods** [37]. **2025: USD 105.2M**, of which **USD 86M came from digital goods** (+94% YoY). This is from Leo Faria's end-of-year letter, 16–17 Dec 2025, on X [38] (the primary is robots-blocked, so it was read via Sheep Esports [39] and Dot Esports [40]). For 2026, Riot said "over $100 million" [41].
- **VCT 2027 partnership (two-year cycle from 2027):** Riot's own 2027 announcement (8 Apr 2026) lists partner benefits as a **base payment**, a **performance bonus**, **team capsules** and direct seeding. It also gives total prize pools of **">$6M/yr"**, with pools roughly doubling from Cups to Masters to Champions (read via VLR's reproduction [42]; the Riot article is on X [43]). The **dollar range of USD 600K (worst results) to USD 5M (best results) per partner per year** is reported by THESPIKE Brazil, citing Riot's partnership meetings (17 Apr 2026) [9]. It is not confirmed on a Riot page I could read.
- **Non-partners in 2027 (Riot primary, valorantesports.com, 18 Jun 2026):** fixed payouts of **USD 100k** for Kickoff/Cup qualification, **USD 200k** for Masters and **USD 400k** for Champions, plus a USD 100k Game Changers incentive. These come on top of prize money [10]. They serve as a useful **floor for what Riot thinks reaching each tier is worth**.
- **Performance mapping in 2026:** Championship Points decide Champions qualification. SEN finished **10th in Americas with 2 points** (Wikipedia 2026 VCT table) [44] and missed international play for the first time since 2023 [25].

**Confidence:** Structure: primary. 2025 totals: primary via secondary (named Riot exec letter). 2027 dollar range: secondary-reported. Stipend split: secondary with a Riot comment.

## 8. Sponsorship, content, merch and viewership

- **Sentinels (SEC Form C-AR, filed 29 Apr 2026, primary) [16]:** revenue **2024: $5,791,790**, **2025: $6,439,445** (+11%, "due to increases in both digital good sales and expanding of Sponsorships"). Gross profit 2025 $5.91M, operating expenses $9.82M, **net loss ($4.28M)** (2024: ($6.09M)). **Cash $209,269** at year-end 2025, and **$12.0M owed to JAG Future Ventures**. Revenue is not broken down by line. Revenue sources are listed as merch, sponsorship, publisher digital-goods share, league participation payments and prize winnings.
- **Sentinels 2023** (Sheep Esports, from the StartEngine campaign and investor page): revenue **$2.92M**, a self-valuation of **$30M**, a crowdfund that raised **$192,984** against a $1.23M target, and a **2022–23 net loss above $16M** [55]. **Pre-2024.** Implication: revenue **roughly doubled from 2023 to 2024**, the year of TenZ-era Sentinels' Masters Madrid title and the #1 Americas capsule. This is the best available SEN-specific proxy for a "winning/star" uplift. It cannot be split between the effect of star players and the effect of results, and part of it is Riot's digital-goods guarantee and share.
- **Rob Moore (Sheep Esports, 2026):** SEN focuses on sponsorships, merchandise and "bigger shares of digital goods", and targets profitability by 2027 [17].
- **OverActive Media (listed; KOI parent), FY2025:** revenue **CAD 28.5M** (≈ USD 20.1M), net loss CAD 11.4M, reported 28 Apr 2026. Sponsorship is not broken out [56]. There is no VALORANT exposure, so it serves as scale context only.
- **Viewership (Esports Charts):** Americas Stage 1 2026 had the lowest group-stage viewership in VCT Americas history. Peak viewers fell 19.3% and watch time 21.2% YoY, and it was the **first Stage 1 in which the most-watched group-stage game did not feature Sentinels** (14 May 2026) [57]. Americas Stage 2 2026 peaked at **522.8K** (100T vs LOUD grand final) with 24.4M hours watched [58]. Sentinels' all-time peak is **1,687,842** (Masters Madrid 2024) [59]. So the SEN brand draws when it wins and has faded while it loses.

**Confidence:** SEN revenue: primary. Star-player merch/content effect: not found as a number, so illustrative.

## 9. Discount rate / cost of capital

- **Damodaran (NYU Stern), data as of Jan 2026 [60]:** Entertainment cost of equity **7.63%**, WACC **7.13%**. Software (Entertainment) CoE 8.54%, WACC 8.44%. Advertising CoE 9.35%. Total market CoE 8.02%, WACC 6.96%. These are for listed, diversified firms.
- **VC target returns by stage (Damodaran 2009 paper, older data) [61]:** start-up 50–70%, first stage 40–60%, second stage 35–50%, bridge/IPO 25–35%. These include survival risk.
- **Esports-specific:** no citable esports-org WACC was found. SEN is loss-making, financed by related-party debt, and held ~$0.2M cash at the end of 2025 [16], which argues for a rate well above listed-entertainment WACC. However, a one-to-two-year player-contract cash flow does not need a VC-style survival premium.

**Confidence:** Illustrative (generic benchmarks).
